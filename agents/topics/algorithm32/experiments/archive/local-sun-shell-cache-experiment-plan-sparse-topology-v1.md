## Question And Completion Definition

The initial study answers one question:

> Across a frozen family of horizontal local-Sun offsets, how does direct-J2
> position-reconstruction error change with baseline inverse-square shell
> count, realized position-node count, and projected complete resident bytes
> for a scattering-aligned triangular shell cache, compared with a dense
> adjacent-node linear control?

Both caches are scored at the same frozen physical holdout queries against
arbitrary-position binary64 `DirectJ2`.

The initial study is complete only when one frozen characterization family
has:

- a valid direct binary64 authority over every source case;
- a converged shared physical-driver monitor and direct-response audit;
- a converged dense adjacent-node linear control;
- structurally valid shell mappings and every predeclared triangular level;
- one untouched common holdout evaluated only after the realized topologies
  are sealed;
- complete direct-error, node-count, projected-byte, measured slice-resource,
  and descriptive slice-timing records for every source and level;
- immutable source, input, result, and terminal-manifest provenance; and
- one closeout conclusion from the enum defined below.

The study deliberately does not:

- select a cache size, accuracy threshold, resource ceiling, or winner;
- inherit the old `0.1` maximum normalized L-infinity, `0.02` p95 normalized
  RMS, or `83,886,080`-byte resident gates;
- establish a three-dimensional position cache;
- construct J3, J4, cumulative N4, packed production transport, or GPU fields;
- launch a browser, render an image, or use a camera scene as an oracle;
- select an application cache quality profile or animation policy;
- modify production; or
- independently validate the underlying Star physical solver.

A large error, nonmonotone error curve, large cache, or slow slice
implementation is valid characterization output. It is not a rejection gate.

The experiment may never improve a result by:

- adding an artificial ray, no-hit, source-distance, or cache-radius cap;
- using the camera far plane or an observer-centered dome as atmosphere exit;
- scaling the source position, distance, spectrum, radius, or inverse-square
  law;
- changing source radius/epsilon clamps or allowing the atmosphere domain
  inside them;
- shrinking the whole-world disc, slice, atmosphere height, or source-offset
  family;
- changing altitude-based density, extinction, phase, absorption, or ground
  participation;
- dropping transparent atmosphere exit followed by vacuum continuation to the
  complete finite source; or
- changing wavelength, direction, recurrence, or construction controls as a
  hidden budget adjustment.

## Spatial-Placement Guiding Principle

This characterization tests one shared spatial partition of the fixed local-
Sun atmosphere. Shell/cell placement must be driven first by the physical
quantities that vary over position: finite-source distance and inverse-square
change, altitude/density, source-path optical depth, cap thickness,
map-relative geometry, and ground/cap/rim/exit classification. Wavelength and
outgoing direction are field-payload dimensions at each retained node, not
separate spatial coordinates or independent shell meshes.

The frozen `F07`-`F09` monitor design must therefore define one shared
spatial monitor. It may use a conservative scalar envelope derived from the
fixed source spectrum and atmospheric-composition coefficients when that is
needed to protect the most rapidly varying spectral response. Direct binary64
`J2` remains the authority for monitor calibration/audit and reconstruction-
error measurement; it cannot justify a distinct topology per wavelength or
outgoing direction. The exact driver quantities, envelope, any permitted
direct-response correction, and their convergence checks remain `F07`-`F09`
selections and must be frozen before physical work.

## Inverse-Square Shell-Count Guiding Principle

Each shell level is first defined by one integer baseline shell count `N`.
Across the complete valid source-distance range, its `N` intervals are the
equal increments of `1 / rSun^2`; the source irradiance constant cancels.
Thus `N` is the controlled radial-resolution variable: baseline shells are
narrow near the finite source and widen with distance. The frozen ladder must
report `N`, its exact inverse-square boundaries, realized node count, and
projected complete bytes for every source/level.

Near-source bands are smaller because their underlying inverse-square change
per meter is larger, not because the design permits greater variation inside
them. Equal inverse-square intervals have equal absolute unattenuated
available-light change. Any source-path, density, or boundary effect that
breaks that baseline balance is handled only by the declared cell/feature
refinement and verified by direct reconstruction error.

The shared physical-driver monitor may deform a band's physical realization
through source-path transmittance and may require within-band, cross-band, or
boundary-feature cells. It may not silently add or remove baseline radial
bands, replace `N` with per-component shell counts, or create a distinct
wavelength/direction shell set. This characterization measures direct error
and cost as `N` rises; no stage selects an acceptable `N`, error threshold,
or product cache size.

The largest frozen `N` is a deliberately dense practical saturation reference
for the shell representation and packet. It is finite, may not be called a
mathematical lower bound, and never replaces direct `J2` as the authority.
Every lower shell level must report its direct-error curve and successive
refinement delta against that dense level. A small final delta is descriptive
only: it cannot stop the campaign early, select `N`, or change the frozen
ladder. A ladder that does not reach practical saturation is extended only in
a new family with a fresh holdout.

## Shell-Method Test-Design Principle

The pure inverse-square construction is the required baseline shell method.
An attenuation-aware source-path deformation is a separately named and
predeclared alternative method, never a post-result adjustment to the
baseline. A family contains exactly one of those methods. Comparing them
requires separate family IDs, frozen ladders, and fresh untouched holdouts;
viewed holdout output from either family becomes regression evidence and may
not tune the other.

Every method family retains the same complete physical coverage: centered and
off-center sources; near-center and near-rim source cases; ground, cap,
source-axis, map-center, and both-rim features; and every required joint
reflection control. The method's controlled variable is its frozen increasing
shell-count ladder `N`; within-band and cross-band refinement use only frozen
deterministic feature rules. Each family compares its shell cache with the
dense linear control at the same kind of untouched direct binary64 queries.
The dense final shell level is descriptive saturation evidence, never an
oracle, a stop rule, or a cache-selection rule.

## Structured Cache-Space Representation Principle

The cache representation is a direct structured grid in `(u, v, s)`: inverse-
square shell layer, local ground-to-exit coordinate, and local arc coordinate
on that shell/altitude row. Geometry performs one analytic physical-to-cache
transform, computes a direct structured cell address, and returns a fixed
stencil. It performs no tetrahedron/triangle containment search, tree walk,
or variable-length locator traversal.

Ground, cap, rim, source-axis, and outside-domain behavior are deterministic
consequences of the selected physical boundary and coordinate transform. A
valid query uses an interior fixed stencil or a declared fixed boundary
stencil; an exited ray continues through vacuum and performs no cache lookup.
The exact dimensions, direct-index equations, row-bound/validity layout, and
boundary stencils are representation inputs, not adaptive runtime choices.

This replaces the earlier sparse tetrahedral candidate description. The
remaining Stage 4 schema/action details that name triangles, tetrahedra, or a
coarse locator must be read as superseded design text and are to be rewritten
as structured-grid validation before `LSC-S1` can materialize a family.

Source offsets are alternative physical configurations. Their resident bytes
are reported separately and never summed as simultaneously resident caches.

Identity construction is acyclic:

1. The approved `local-sun-shell-cache-family-policy-vK.json` owns identity,
   ordering, closure, and report rules but contains no generated family hash
   or generated family-manifest hash.
2. Each retained child input has its own `schemaVersion` and `inputId`, but no
   `familyId` and no self hash.
3. Its identity is the SHA-256 of its exact retained raw bytes.
4. The generated `local-sun-shell-cache-family-manifest-vK.json` separately
   pins the approval register, then lists `{path, role, rawSha256}` entries
   for the selection authority, family policy, artifact schemas, approved
   owner inputs, and inherited prerequisites in
   lexicographic repository-relative path order. It contains no family ID and
   does not list itself.
5. `familyDefinitionSha256` is the SHA-256 of the exact raw family-manifest
   bytes.
6. `familyId` is
   `local-sun-shell-cache-characterization-<familyDefinitionSha256>`.
7. A realized child-ID record is serialized as compact UTF-8 JSON plus one
   trailing line feed, with keys in this fixed order:
   `schemaVersion`, `familyDefinitionSha256`, `sourceCaseId`, `cacheKind`,
   `levelId`, `nodeArtifactSha256`, `topologyArtifactSha256`,
   `packetArtifactSha256`. Its raw SHA-256 is `childId`.

## Data Independence Contract

| Partition | May influence | May not influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `regression` | Physical-driver estimates, one shared monitor, mapping, shell placement, and predeclared deterministic refinement decisions; a direct response may enter only through the frozen shared scalar audit/envelope rule | Independent accuracy claims or a distinct wavelength/direction topology |
| `controlConvergence` | Mechanical selection of one retained linear level by the frozen rule | Shell placement or independent accuracy claims |
| `structural` | Topology-independent feature cases plus topology-relative probes generated by a frozen rule, used for coverage, ownership, exact-node, constant, continuity, locator, chart, and attribution checks | Accuracy acceptance or method tuning |
| `holdout` | Direct-error characterization of every frozen shell and linear level | Any method, parameter, level, topology, pairing, metric, or retained-control decision |

The holdout generator, seed, strata, count, and exact realized physical
queries are frozen and hashed in `LSC-S1`, before either topology exists.
Coordinates need not be encrypted. Independence is enforced mechanically:

- fitting, linear-control, monitor, topology, and preflight modules cannot
  import the holdout file;
- `holdoutDirectJ2EvaluationCount` must remain zero through `LSC-S5`;
- the `LSC-S5` ledger must record `realized-topology-sealed`;
- `LSC-S6` may evaluate holdout direct values only after verifying that event
  and every sealed hash; and
- after any holdout value is viewed, any numerical or candidate change
  requires a new `familyId` and a completely fresh holdout.

`LSC-S1` materializes only topology-independent structural feature cases. It
freezes, but does not execute, the rule for producing exact-node, shell,
control-line, edge, cell-interior, and monitor-extremum probes from realized
topology. `LSC-S3` and `LSC-S4` materialize and hash those derived probes;
`LSC-S5` seals them. An accidental coordinate collision with holdout rejects
the family. Neither dataset is edited to remove the collision.

There is no separately named validation set. Anything other than the untouched
holdout is regression, control-convergence, or structural evidence.

## Interstage Artifact Contract

A later stage may consume an earlier result only through the exact artifact
path and raw SHA-256 covered by that accepted run's verified terminal
manifest. It may not recover a value from `report.md`, a plot, console output,
or an unmanifested temporary file.

`local-sun-shell-cache-artifact-schemas-v1.json`, approved under `F22`, owns
the exact key order, type, unit, enum, array order, binary axis order, and
completeness constraints for every schema ID below. `LSC-S1` validates that
registry but does not invent it. The common-key rule below applies only to
F22-governed S2-S6 interstage, table, binary-descriptor, and diagnostic
artifacts named by this plan:

- is UTF-8 `JSON.stringify(value, null, 2)` plus one trailing line feed;
- starts with keys `schemaVersion`, `schemaId`, `stageId`,
  `familyDefinitionSha256`, `familyId`, `producerRunId`, and
  `sourceClosureSha256` in that order;
- uses schema-declared array order rather than object-property enumeration as
  evidence order; and
- contains repository-relative paths, byte lengths, and raw SHA-256 values
  for every referenced payload.

Shared run-record files retain the lane's existing schemas and are exempt from
those seven common leading keys:

```text
result.json
inputs.json
provenance.json
environment.json
references/manifest.json
artifact-manifest.json
```

`report.md` and `command.txt` are non-JSON shared run files. S0's
`tables/gates.json`, `diagnostics/execution-ledger.json`, and
`diagnostics/import-closure.json` use the exact special S0 schemas in its
stage card and are also exempt. No other experiment-owned S2-S6 table or
diagnostic receives an implicit exemption.

Every binary DirectJ2 packet artifact is headerless little-endian IEEE-754
binary64. Its JSON descriptor freezes shape, axis order, packet-component
order from `F05`, query/node order, byte length, and raw SHA-256. A binary
artifact with no matching descriptor, or a descriptor whose expected byte
length does not equal the payload, is invalid evidence.

Let `P` be the attributable aggregate attempts that name the prior input.
Select the transition by this first-match rule:

1. `scanner-anomaly-closure` when the fresh scan contains any priority-1
   through priority-4 scanner anomaly and either `P` is nonempty or
   `newScannerAnomalyKeys` is nonempty. Every leaf and aggregate attempt must
   be terminal-valid, released by its exact decision-bound leaf approval, or
   cited by the exact quiescence-only record. The new input is permanently
   `closure-only` under the first scanner issue. For `P = []`, the nonempty
   set is mandatory: an anomaly already retained by the unattempted prior
   input cannot be used to abandon it again.
2. `terminal-attempt` when `P` has exactly one row and it is terminal-valid
   with `aggregate-execution-failed` or
   `aggregate-execution-interrupted`.
3. `quiesced-attempt` when `P` has exactly one row, it is allocated-only,
   initialized-invalid, initialized-unfinalized, or finalized-invalid, and
   the exact quiescence-only record cites it.
4. `explicit-terminal-close` when `P = []`, no scanner anomaly or
   terminal-valid rejected leaf outranks it, the prior input is
   `complete-characterization` with null closure authority, and a newly
   supplied exact explicit terminal-close authority validates. The same
   authority cannot be introduced twice.
5. `same-anchor-reporting-repair` when `P = []`, no new scanner anomaly or
   explicit-close introduction exists, both attempt-evidence hashes are
   equal, both quiescence-semantics hashes are equal, and the exact
   same-anchor source comparison in `Aggregate Execution` proves a nonempty
   authorized reporting-only difference between the immediate prior and
   current decisions.
6. `retained-evidence-refresh` when `P = []`, no new scanner anomaly,
   explicit-close introduction, or decision-source change exists; at least
   one attempt-evidence or quiescence-semantics hash differs; run IDs, slugs,
   attribution identities, and ordering are unchanged; and the only row
   changes are monotone evidence advances. A null field may acquire its
   verified value, an attempt state may advance through the declared
   five-state order, and a newly observed terminal pair may fill its terminal
   fields. A previously verified non-null path, hash, identity, status, or
   timestamp may not disappear or change, and no prior anomaly key may
   disappear. Activity after a prior quiescence record's
   `executionVerifiedAt` is not a retained-evidence refresh: it creates the
   applicable `post-quiescence-activity` scanner anomaly and selects
   `scanner-anomaly-closure`. A replacement record may prove the now-complete
   required attempt set inactive so that successor preparation can finish,
   but it cannot erase the retained anomaly or restore complete mode.
7. Otherwise preparation fails
   `PREVIOUS_AGGREGATION_INPUT_UNATTEMPTED` for zero attempts,
   `PREVIOUS_AGGREGATION_INPUT_AMBIGUOUS` for multiple attempts, or
   `PREVIOUS_AGGREGATE_ATTEMPT_INVALID` for one attempt that satisfies none

`releasedLeafApprovals` is the exact list of decision-bound nonnumerical

nonterminal leaf attempts as inactive. Rows are ordered by approval path;
paths/hashes are unique. Each `releasedRunIds` array is nonempty, contains

recomputes this list, and the aggregate requires byte identity with it.

Assignment is per run and first-match deterministic. For each otherwise
unquiesced leaf attempt in allocation order:

1. If one or more later valid-input leaf attempts historically consumed
   approvals that cite it, assign the approval consumed by the earliest such
   leaf run; break an impossible same-run tie by lower repair ordinal.
2. Otherwise, assign the highest repair ordinal in the verified campaign-tip
   history whose approval cites the run and is currently valid for this scan.
3. If neither exists, leave the run for the exact top-level quiescence
   authority.

After assignment, group run IDs by approval path/hash and serialize the rows
under the ordering rule above. A replacement approval's complete prior-run
list therefore cannot create a discretionary choice between old and new
approvals.

Decision-token rules therefore win over simultaneous source-token defects.
No parser reports more than one `attributionIssue` for a directory.

State classification uses the enum order above and stops at the first
unsatisfied condition. An `allocated-only` row has every field after
`attemptState` through `repairApprovalRawSha256` null; its two time fields are
null. An `initialized-invalid` row requires non-null `inputsPath`;
`inputsRawSha256` is non-null when the bytes are readable and null only when
the path cannot be read. It keeps terminal, decision, result, manifest,
closure, and time fields null. A readable `inputs.json` whose
`closurePrecheck.status` is `precheck-error`, whose descriptors do not match
the independently recomputed closure, or whose expected hashes do not match
the anchor is `initialized-invalid`; none of its three closure hashes may be
populated.

An `initialized-unfinalized` row requires verified input, decision,
source-closure, numerical-source-closure, and full numerical-closure fields,
has `startedAt = inputs.json.capturedAt`, and has null `completedAt`,
terminal, result, and manifest fields. The scanner independently recomputes
both numerical descriptors before treating the input as verified; a
pre-start candidate descriptor alone is insufficient. A `finalized-invalid`
row requires verified input, decision, all three closure fields, its verified
`startedAt`, null `completedAt`, and a non-null path for every terminal file
that exists. A readable file has a non-null raw hash; an unreadable or absent
one has a null hash or null path/hash respectively. `terminalStatus` is null
because no unmanifested declaration is a terminal status. A `terminal-valid`
row requires non-null, verified input, decision, terminal, result, manifest,
source-closure, numerical-source-closure, and full numerical-closure fields.
Both numerical hashes must equal the retained anchor for that source. Its
`startedAt` is the byte-identical
`inputs.json.capturedAt`/`result.json.startedAt` instant and its `completedAt`
is the manifest-covered `result.json.completedAt` instant. The repair fields
are non-null only when that attempt used the approved repair path.

For every terminal-valid attempt, the directory name,
`inputs.json.runId`, `result.json.run.id`, `artifact-manifest.json.runId`,
and aggregation `runId` are byte-identical. `result.json.status`,
`artifact-manifest.json.status`, and aggregation `terminalStatus` are
byte-identical, as are the result/manifest/aggregation `completedAt` values.
Every trusted instant must match
`^[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}T[0-9]{2}:[0-9]{2}:[0-9]{2}\.[0-9]{3}Z$`,
parse to a finite millisecond value, and round-trip through
`new Date(value).toISOString()` byte-identically. Terminal validity requires
`startedAt <= completedAt`; any mismatch follows finalized-invalid state
rather than letting the scanner choose one file as authority.

`campaignOrderIssue` and `campaignOrderPriorRunId` are evaluated after the
evidence-state fields. The scanner processes attributed attempts in global
run-allocation order and applies the following first-match rule:

1. `post-aggregation-seal-attempt` when the earliest schema-valid
   aggregation input for this campaign exists and its retained leaf census
   does not contain this attempt's run ID. The prior run is null because the
   immutable input file, not a run, is the sealing evidence.
2. `post-quiescence-activity` when an earlier schema-valid aggregation input
   cited this run through its exact quiescence authority and a fresh scan
   observes any write later than the cited `executionVerifiedAt`, an advanced
   attempt state, a newly non-null evidence field, or a changed previously
   non-null evidence path/hash/status/timestamp relative to that input's
   retained row. The earliest breached input version owns the issue. The
   prior run is null because the breached authority record, not another run,
   is the ordering evidence. A replacement quiescence record cannot erase
   this issue.
3. `post-retry-release-activity` when a historically valid nonnumerical
   repair approval that cites this run has been consumed by either a later
   valid-input leaf attempt whose retained start/input
   `repairApprovalPath`/hash proves that the approval verified at admission,
   or an earlier schema-valid aggregation input whose
   `releasedLeafApprovals` row proves that the approval verified when that
   input sealed its scan.
   A fresh scan must also observe either any run-directory write later than
   that approval's `priorExecutionVerifiedAt` or an
   `attemptState`/`terminalStatus` different from the approval's retained row.
   Select the earliest consuming leaf run or aggregation-input version, then
   the lowest repair ordinal on a tie. The prior run is null because the
   breached approval, not another run, is the evidence. Mere creation or
   history inclusion of an unused approval does not activate this rule. Once
   activated, no later decision, approval, completion, or retry can erase the
   issue. Historical validity is not revoked when the consuming retry later
   becomes canonical; that event only prevents future reuse of the approval.
4. `stale-leaf-start-precheck` when a schema-valid retained
   `leafStartPrecheck.attemptProjectionSha256` does not match the exact
   lower-run-number projection; the prior run is the first differing prior
   attempt, or null when the mismatch is structural. This rule is skipped
   when no schema-valid precheck exists; allocated-only and
   initialized-invalid state then remains governed by the later rules.
5. `campaign-already-rejected` when an earlier terminal-valid rejected leaf
   exists; the prior run is that first rejected run.
6. `prior-source-not-accepted-at-start` when the attempt's row is later than
   the first campaign row that lacked a canonical accepted leaf at the
   attempt's `startedAt`; the prior run is the latest attempt in the blocking
   earlier row, or null when that row has no attempt.
7. `overlapping-attempt` when an earlier valid-input leaf interval had begun
   but had no verified completion no later than this attempt's `startedAt`;
   the prior run is the earliest such open interval. A prior interval named by
   the current attempt's valid nonnumerical retry approval is closed for this
   ordering test at `priorExecutionVerifiedAt`, but later activity by that
   released run has already received the higher-priority
   `post-retry-release-activity` issue.
8. `source-already-accepted` when the same row already had a canonical
   accepted leaf completed before this attempt began; the prior run is that
   canonical leaf.
9. `prior-attempt-not-terminal-at-start` when the same row had an earlier
   allocated-only, initialized-invalid, initialized-unfinalized, or
   finalized-invalid attempt and no verified completion instant, unless the
   current attempt's valid
   nonnumerical retry approval cites it; the prior run is the earliest
   uncited attempt.
10. Otherwise both fields are null.

For an allocated-only or initialized-invalid attempt with no trusted
`startedAt`, the seal rule still uses retained-census membership, the
quiescence/retry-release activity rules still compare retained evidence and
observed writes, the stale-precheck rule is applied only when a valid precheck
is present, the
overlap rule is not inferred, and the campaign-rejected, prior-source,
source-already-accepted, and prior-attempt rules use numeric allocation
position. Every earlier source row must already have a canonical
accepted leaf and every earlier same-row attempt must already be
terminal-valid failed or interrupted or be cited by the current attempt's
valid nonnumerical retry approval. The attempt itself may remain order-valid,
but any later attempt is blocked by it until one of those two release
conditions exists. A terminal-valid failed/interrupted retry is order-valid
only when its predecessor's `completedAt <= startedAt`. An approved
nonterminal retry is order-valid only when
`priorExecutionVerifiedAt <= startedAt`. Equality at millisecond resolution
is accepted only when the retained allocation-guard projection proves the
predecessor or release was visible under the lock before allocation. Without
that guard proof, equality remains overlap.

An attempt with a non-null campaign-order issue can never become canonical.
The first issue in allocation order is deterministic campaign-invalid
evidence; all attempts remain listed. This post-run rule is defense-in-depth
for a directory created by pre-contract, corrupt, or nonconforming code; the
atomic guard prevents that race for conforming commands.

The aggregate independently scans allocated run-directory names and every
readable Stage 6 leaf `inputs.json`; it must include every attempt whose slug
names the selected `c<K>` campaign token, including allocated-only and
malformed attempts. A directory is evidence-invalid when its slug names that
campaign token but does not name exactly one expected source case. Other
campaign tokens are outside this aggregation universe. Validly attributed
attempts go under their expected row. Invalidly attributed attempts go in
`unattributedAttempts` in global numeric allocation order; they are never
discarded or forced into a convenient source row. Each uses the same
state/nullable-field rules as a source-row attempt.

The canonical leaf is the first order-valid `terminal-valid` accepted attempt
whose family, campaign, seal, numerical closure, and any required repair
approval verify, including both anchored numerical hashes, provided no
earlier attempt for that row is terminal-valid rejected.
A terminal-valid rejected attempt closes the campaign; any later source-leaf
allocation under that campaign is evidence-invalid and can never become
canonical. A later faster or lower-memory accepted attempt may never replace
the first valid accepted attempt.

The same preparation scan also inventories every run directory whose slug
begins `lsc-s6-aggregate-c<K>-` for the selected campaign. These rows form
`aggregateAttempts` in global allocation order; they are never leaf rows.
The suffix is attributable by this first-match rule:

0. If the earliest schema-valid aggregation input that censused this run
   recorded any non-null `attributionIssue`, copy that exact issue and its
   nullable `aggregationInputVersion` forever and keep
   `aggregationInputPath`/hash null. Later target versions, input creation,
   or file correction never reclassify the run.
1. Otherwise, no `a<token>` suffix is
   `missing-aggregation-input-version`.
2. A present token that is not canonical base-10 `[1-9][0-9]*`, exceeds the
   positive-safe-integer range, or has leading zeroes is
   `invalid-aggregation-input-version`.
3. A valid `M` is `preexisting-aggregation-input-attempt` when either:
   the scanner is preparing the absent target aggregation input `v<M>` and
   this attempt already exists; or the earliest schema-valid retained input
   that censused this run lists that issue.
   The row retains `aggregationInputVersion = M` but null
   `aggregationInputPath`/hash. Inclusion in the target's own census is the
   immutable proof that the attempt preceded that input; later existence of
   the file never reclassifies the row.
4. A valid `M` whose exact
   `cases/local-sun-shell-cache-stage6-c<K>-aggregation-v<M>.json` is absent,
   unreadable, schema/hash-invalid, or bound to another family/campaign is
   `unknown-aggregation-input-version`.
5. Otherwise `aggregationInputVersion = M`, `attributionIssue = null`, and
   the row is bound to that exact retained input path/hash.

The target version in item 3 comes only from
`targetAggregationInputPath`; it is not selected from a slug. This makes a
future-version bypass deterministic: if `a<M>` exists before `v<M>`,
preparation records the issue in `v<M>`, and every rescan reproduces it
instead of changing it to a normal attribution after `v<M>` is created.
Rows with a non-null attribution issue still receive the five-state evidence
classification from their run directory, but no later-written input can
erase the issue or supply a trusted aggregation-input binding.

Aggregate attribution and evidence state are orthogonal and use this exact
field policy:

- `missing-aggregation-input-version` and
  `invalid-aggregation-input-version` have
  `aggregationInputVersion = null`.
- `preexisting-aggregation-input-attempt`,
  `unknown-aggregation-input-version`, and a null attribution issue retain
  the canonical parsed positive-safe-integer `M`.
- Only a null attribution issue populates `aggregationInputPath` and
  `aggregationInputRawSha256`, using the exact schema-valid retained `v<M>`
  file. Both fields are populated even for an ordinary `allocated-only` row
  because the immutable aggregate slug identifies a retained input that
  already existed before allocation. Every non-null attribution issue keeps
  both fields null permanently. In particular, later creation of `v<M>` does
  not populate them for a `preexisting-aggregation-input-attempt`.

Evidence-state classification then inspects the run directory independently
of that attribution result. A non-null attribution issue is never repaired
or made authoritative by valid-looking run files, but those files still
receive their reproducible evidence state. The exact state matrix is:

| State | `inputsPath` / hash | decision path/hash | result and manifest path/hash | status/conclusion | times |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `allocated-only` | both null | both null | all four null | both null | both null |
| `initialized-invalid` | expected run-relative path; hash iff a readable regular file | both null | all four null | both null | both null |
| `initialized-unfinalized` | both non-null and verified | both non-null and equal the verified input binding | all four null | both null | verified `startedAt`; null `completedAt` |
| `finalized-invalid` | both non-null and verified | both non-null and equal the verified input binding | each existing expected file has its path and a hash iff readable/regular; an absent file has null path/hash | both null | verified `startedAt`; null `completedAt` |
| `terminal-valid` | both non-null and verified | both non-null and equal the verified input binding | all four non-null and manifest-verified | exact permitted pair below | verified `startedAt` and `completedAt` |

Here “all four” means `resultPath`, `resultRawSha256`,
`artifactManifestPath`, and `artifactManifestRawSha256`. A valid aggregate
run input must bind one exact schema-valid aggregation input, S6 decision,
campaign, numerical anchor, and `aggregateStartPrecheck`; it has no source or
numerical-packet closure. For a non-null attribution issue, that internally
named aggregation input may participate only in state verification and is
not copied into the row's null attribution-owned
`aggregationInputPath`/hash fields. A state cannot skip forward: the first
unsatisfied matrix row determines it. `aggregateOrderIssue` and its two prior
fields are computed only after this complete matrix has been serialized.

For a terminal-valid aggregate row, directory name, `inputs.json.runId`,
`result.json.run.id`, `artifact-manifest.json.runId`, and aggregation `runId`
are byte-identical. Status and completed-time equality and canonical timestamp
rules are the same as for terminal-valid leaves. Its
`terminalConclusion`/status pair must be exactly one of:

```text
accepted     / characterization-complete
rejected     / evidence-invalid
failed       / execution-incomplete
failed       / aggregate-execution-failed
interrupted  / aggregate-execution-interrupted
```

The first three are terminal family conclusions and are terminal-valid only
under this exact input-mode/authority/artifact mapping:

- `complete-characterization`, null closure authority, and the exact complete
  aggregate artifact set require
  `accepted / characterization-complete`;
- `closure-only` with
  `terminal-valid-evidence-rejection`,
  `deterministic-attribution-rejection`,
  `deterministic-campaign-order-rejection`,
  `deterministic-aggregate-attribution-rejection`, or
  `deterministic-aggregate-order-rejection`, plus the exact closure artifact
  set, requires `rejected / evidence-invalid`; and
- `closure-only` with `explicit-project-terminal-close` plus the exact
  closure artifact set requires `failed / execution-incomplete`.

Any cross-mode pair, wrong closure kind, missing/extra mode-specific artifact,
or non-null closure authority in complete mode is `finalized-invalid` for
the three terminal family conclusions.
The last two pairs are attempt conclusions and may occur under either
retained mode after execution begins. Their exact terminal-valid artifact
envelope is the eight standard aggregate artifacts plus a complete prefix,
possibly empty, of that mode's additional artifact list in the displayed
`Required Aggregate Artifacts` order. Every retained prefix member must be
schema-valid and manifest-covered; no later member, unlisted artifact, or
atomic temporary may exist. The result records the last completed phase.
These attempt envelopes make no family claim, contribute no partial table to
a successor, and permit only a reporting retry with unchanged leaf/result
bytes. Any other status/conclusion or artifact shape is
`finalized-invalid`.

`aggregateOrderIssue` is evaluated in allocation order by this first-match
rule:

1. `post-successor-aggregation-input-attempt` for an attributable attempt
   bound to input `vM` when the least schema-valid retained input `vN` with
   `N > M` omits this run ID from its immutable `aggregateAttempts` census.
   That omission proves the attempt was allocated only after the successor
   input had already sealed its scan. Set
   `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion = N` and
   `aggregateOrderPriorRunId = null`.
2. `post-quiescence-activity` when an earlier schema-valid aggregation input
   cited this aggregate run through its exact quiescence authority and a
   fresh scan observes any write later than `executionVerifiedAt`, an
   advanced state, a newly non-null evidence field, or a changed previously
   non-null path/hash/status/conclusion/timestamp relative to that input's
   retained row. The earliest breached input version is
   `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion`; `aggregateOrderPriorRunId = null`.
   A replacement quiescence record cannot erase the issue.
3. `stale-aggregate-start-precheck` when a schema-valid retained
   `aggregateStartPrecheck` does not equal the exact lower-run-number
   aggregate projection used by the allocation guard. This rule is skipped
   when no schema-valid precheck exists; allocated-only and
   initialized-invalid evidence then remains eligible for exact quiescence
   rather than becoming an inferred order issue. Set
   `aggregateOrderPriorRunId` to the first differing lower-run-number
   aggregate attempt, or null when the mismatch is only structural or
   top-level; set `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion = null`.
4. `campaign-already-closed` when an earlier authoritative terminal-valid
   aggregate emitted a terminal family conclusion. Set
   `aggregateOrderPriorRunId` to the first such closing run in allocation
   order and `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion = null`.
5. `aggregation-input-already-attempted` when an earlier attributable
   aggregate names the same exact aggregation-input path/hash. Set
   `aggregateOrderPriorRunId` to the first such run in allocation order and
   `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion = null`.
6. `overlapping-aggregate-attempt` when an earlier aggregate had neither a
   terminal-valid completion nor exact quiescence evidence no later than
   this attempt's trusted `startedAt`. Set `aggregateOrderPriorRunId` to the
   earliest such open run and `aggregateOrderPriorInputVersion = null`. Skip
   this rule when the current row has no trusted `startedAt`.
7. Otherwise the issue and both prior fields are null.

An allocated-only attempt that
names an older input after a successor exists is therefore order-invalid even
though it has no `aggregateStartPrecheck`. An aggregate-attribution or
aggregate-order issue can never contribute a
characterization. It becomes deterministic closure evidence under the mode
priority below. A conforming allocation guard prevents every order issue;
these rules preserve deterministic interpretation of pre-contract, corrupt,
or bypassed directories.

An aggregate terminal family conclusion is authoritative only when the row is
`terminal-valid`, `attributionIssue = null`,
`aggregateOrderIssue = null`, its exact aggregation input and guarded start
precheck verify, and its result/manifest pair has one of the three terminal
family status/conclusion pairs above. Before applying mode priority, any
prior authoritative conclusion makes preparation fail
`CAMPAIGN_ALREADY_CLOSED`; no later aggregation input or aggregate run is
legal. A terminal-looking result on an attribution- or order-invalid
aggregate is not campaign authority. Its scanner issue retains higher
priority and must be closed deterministically.

`aggregationMode` is not an executor choice. The scanner applies this exact
priority:

1. A nonempty `unattributedAttempts` array requires `closure-only` with
   `deterministic-attribution-rejection`.
2. Otherwise, the first aggregate row with a non-null `attributionIssue`
   requires `closure-only` with
   `deterministic-aggregate-attribution-rejection`.
3. Otherwise, the first non-null campaign-order issue in allocation order
   requires `closure-only` with
   `deterministic-campaign-order-rejection`.
4. Otherwise, the first non-null aggregate-order issue in allocation order
   requires `closure-only` with
   `deterministic-aggregate-order-rejection`.
5. Otherwise, the first terminal-valid rejected leaf in allocation order
   requires `closure-only` with `terminal-valid-evidence-rejection`.
6. Otherwise, a valid explicit terminal-close record requires `closure-only`
   with `explicit-project-terminal-close`.
7. Otherwise, one canonical accepted run for every source row requires
   `complete-characterization` and null closure authority.
8. Otherwise, the campaign is retryable/incomplete; preparation fails without
   creating an aggregation input.

`closure-only` permits null canonical runs and calculates no characterization
curve. `complete-characterization` permits a non-null quiescence authority
only when every cited row is an aggregate attempt; leaf incompleteness cannot
be waived into a characterization. Every nonterminal historical leaf must
already be quiesced by its valid decision-bound nonnumerical retry approval.

priority is applied. An invalid supplied record fails preparation; the
scanner never ignores it and falls through. `--closure-authority` is required
only when priority 6 selects `explicit-project-terminal-close` and is
forbidden for scanner-derived closure or complete characterization.
`--quiescence-authority` is required exactly when one or more scanned
attempts remain nonquiescent after terminal-valid completion and
decision-bound leaf retry approvals are applied. It is forbidden when that
exact set is empty. Closure selection and execution quiescence are separate
facts and never share one record.

### Stage 6 Atomic Quiescence And Aggregation Seal

Stage 6 extends shared run allocation only through these backward-compatible
hooks:

```text
allocateRunDirectory({
  runsDirectory,
  slug,
  allocationOptions,
  allocationGuard = null
})

withRunAllocationLock({
  runsDirectory,
  allocationOptions,
  operation
})

startRunRecord({
  ...existingArguments,
  allocationGuard = null,
  allocationGuardResultKey = null
})
```

`src/run-record/index.js` re-exports `withRunAllocationLock`; no existing
export changes identity or behavior.

`allocateRunDirectory` calls `allocationGuard` exactly once while holding the
existing `.run-allocation-lock`, after deriving the normalized slug and next
run identity but before creating the directory or changing
`.last-run-number`. It passes a frozen value with keys
`runsDirectory`, `slug`, `number`, `numberText`, and `name` in that order. A
throw creates no directory and changes no allocator state. A successful
guard result must be JSON-serializable and is returned as
`allocationGuardResult`.

`withRunAllocationLock` resolves the same runs directory, acquires that exact
same lock implementation, invokes and awaits `operation` exactly once, and
releases in `finally`. The operation receives only
`{runsDirectory}` and may perform retained-input/scanner I/O; physical,
candidate, metric, resource, timing, and test execution is forbidden while
the lock is held. The lock owner refreshes the lock-directory modification
time every `1,000 ms`; the existing `10,000 ms` stale threshold and
`30,000 ms` acquisition timeout remain unchanged. Heartbeat start, refresh,
stop, owner-token verification, and stale-reclaim races receive focused
tests. No owner may reclaim a lock whose heartbeat is current.

`startRunRecord` passes its optional guard to the allocator. When
`allocationGuardResultKey` is non-null, it must be a nonempty key absent from
the supplied `inputs`; after allocation the writer inserts the guard result
under that key in a fresh shallow copy, revalidates serializability, and
writes that copy. Stage 6 leaves use exactly
`allocationGuardResultKey = "leafStartPrecheck"`; Stage 6 aggregates use
exactly `allocationGuardResultKey = "aggregateStartPrecheck"`. For guarded allocation,
`startedAt` is captured immediately after allocation and before the first
initial-record write; callers may not supply a timestamp override. Existing
unguarded callers, arguments, results, lock timing, and run bytes remain
unchanged except for the heartbeat's internal temporary-lock behavior.

The Stage 6 leaf allocation guard, while holding the allocation lock:

1. calls `assertStage6LeafStartEligible` for the exact source/decision;
2. returns that helper's complete value; and
3. lets allocation proceed only on success.

After `startRunRecord` completes, the leaf calls `withRunAllocationLock` and
rechecks the same aggregation-seal predicate before its independent closure
verification or any physical import. If sealed, it finalizes failed with all
physical counters zero. If unsealed, it records
`stage6-leaf-physical-admission` in the execution ledger and begins work only
after releasing the lock.

The preparation command first computes its candidate scan outside the lock.
It then calls `withRunAllocationLock`; inside that operation it rescans,
requires byte equality with the candidate, proves the exact output version
and `priorAggregationInput` chain, and exclusively creates the aggregation
input. The first valid aggregation input for a campaign is that campaign's
permanent `aggregation-sealed` event. No later leaf allocation is legal, even
when an aggregate fails and a later report-only aggregation-input version is
needed.

Before creating any aggregation-input version, preparation requires every
allocated campaign leaf and every earlier aggregate attempt to be quiescent.
An attempt is quiescent when it has a terminal-valid `completedAt`, when a
later valid nonnumerical leaf-retry approval cites it and supplies the
accepted `priorExecutionVerifiedAt`, or when the exact quiescence-only record
cites it. The first two forms need no top-level quiescence authority. An
allocated-only, initialized-invalid, initialized-unfinalized, or
finalized-invalid attempt is not presumed inactive merely because its files
are incomplete. Without exact quiescence evidence, preparation exits
`CAMPAIGN_ATTEMPT_ACTIVE` and creates no input.

For a second or later aggregation input, preparation applies the exact
`priorAggregationInput.transitionKind` first-match rule above while holding
the allocation lock. A scanner-anomaly transition may close a zero-attempt
input only when the freshly computed anomaly-key difference is nonempty; it
may therefore close a bypassed post-seal leaf, a delayed old-input aggregate,
or newly revealed malformed/stale evidence, but it cannot repeat the same
retained anomaly. An explicit-terminal-close transition introduces one new
valid authority exactly once. A retained-evidence transition requires the
two exact evidence hashes, stable attempt identities/order, and monotone
evidence advance. A same-anchor reporting transition may supersede an
unattempted input only after its old and current decision-bound focused
source inventories pass the exact reporting-only comparison below. Every
attempt required by the selected transition must be terminal,
decision-released, or cited by the exact quiescence-only record. A prior
authoritative terminal family conclusion blocks every transition.

Every aggregate start uses the same allocation lock. Its guard calls
`assertStage6AggregateStartEligible`, stores the exact result as
`aggregateStartPrecheck`, and permits allocation only on success. After
initialization and before reading leaf payloads, the aggregate rescans under
the lock with its own run excluded and requires byte identity with its
retained aggregation input. Thus a delayed old-input command and the second
of two concurrent commands both fail before reporting work.

This ordering closes both races:

- if leaf allocation wins the lock, preparation sees the new attempt and
  cannot seal while it is active; and
- if preparation wins, it creates the permanent seal before releasing the
  lock, so the leaf allocation guard fails.

For aggregate-versus-aggregate contention, the first allocation becomes an
allocated-only row before the lock is released. The second guard therefore
fails `AGGREGATION_INPUT_ALREADY_ATTEMPTED`; it cannot allocate a competing
closeout run. A later reporting-only retry is possible only through the next
immutable aggregation-input version after the prior attempt is terminal or
explicitly quiesced.

A process that allocated immediately before preparation but had not finished
its initial record is still nonquiescent and blocks sealing. The leaf's
post-start locked recheck is a second invariant, not a substitute for that
rule. Once sealed, aggregate rescans cannot race a conforming new leaf, so
their final byte-equality check has no scan-to-finalize leaf-allocation
window. Preparation and aggregate focused tests must force both lock
leaf/preparation interleavings and both aggregate-allocation orders and prove
these outcomes without physical work.

The initial timing/dataflow choice is fixed:

- `LSC-S3` evaluates DirectJ2 once per distinct physical linear-node identity
  in the union of all frozen linear levels for each source, then materializes
  every child packet from that accepted union. Its linear-node count is the
  summed per-source union cardinality, not the sum of nested child sizes.
  It evaluates each distinct frozen control-convergence query packet once per
  source and shares that authority across all linear levels.
- `LSC-S4` likewise evaluates DirectJ2 once per distinct physical shell-node
  identity in the union of all realized shell levels for each source, then
  materializes child packets from that union. Its shell-node count is the
  summed per-source union cardinality. Neither stage deduplicates across
  different source configurations.
- `LSC-S3` and `LSC-S4` retain immutable node-packet artifacts and their raw
  hashes for every child.
- After topology-derived probes are materialized, `LSC-S3` evaluates DirectJ2
  once per distinct physical query in the union of its topology-independent
  and linear topology-relative structural probes for each source.
  `LSC-S4` does the same for the union of its topology-independent and shell
  topology-relative structural probes. Each stage retains one ordered direct
  structural packet per source plus per-level lookup values and pointwise
  errors. Exact-node probes are deliberately re-evaluated as an independent
  packet-semantics/determinism check; they are not aliases of node-packet
  bytes.
- In each of S3 and S4,
  `structuralDirectJ2EvaluationCount` is the sum of the per-source cardinality
  of that stage's deduplicated structural-query union. It is not multiplied by
  levels that reference the same query. Structural error magnitude is
  diagnostic and never selects a level or changes topology.
- `LSC-S5` rebuilds each child exactly once in campaign order. That one
  isolated rebuild recomputes every node packet through DirectJ2, reconstructs
  its topology/mapping/accelerator, compares canonical node, topology, and
  packet descriptor/payload hashes with accepted S3/S4 artifacts, then
  reconstructs the child at the accepted S2 direct-response-audit queries, if
  any, and at the
  deduplicated union of all accepted S3 and S4 structural queries. It compares
  those reconstructions with the manifest-pinned S2/S3/S4 direct packets,
  retains pointwise regression/structural errors, measures allocation
  lifecycle, disposes the child, and proves return to baseline. It performs no
  new DirectJ2 evaluation for those query surfaces and retains parity/error
  records, not duplicate direct packet payloads.
- S5 keeps the S2 scalar-audit surface and structural surface as separately labeled
  regression reports even if an exact physical query occurs in both. Within
  the structural union, ordering is S3 query order followed by previously
  unseen S4 queries. A query present in both structural packets must have
  byte-identical DirectJ2 values; S3 owns the retained value for that duplicate
  and any mismatch records `realized-family-seal-rejected`.
- For `LSC-S5`,
  `linearNodeDirectJ2EvaluationCount = sum(linear child nodeCount)` and
  `shellNodeDirectJ2EvaluationCount = sum(shell child nodeCount)`;
  `physicalDriverEvaluationCount`,
  `directResponseAuditJ2EvaluationCount`,
  `linearConvergenceDirectJ2EvaluationCount`, and
  `structuralDirectJ2EvaluationCount` are zero because accepted packet
  artifacts supply query authority. Sentinel timing-boundary tests execute no
  additional physical build.
- `LSC-S6` recomputes node packets through DirectJ2 for every timed child
  build and requires byte identity with the sealed accepted packet on every
  warm-up and reported repetition. For one source leaf,
  `linearNodeDirectJ2EvaluationCount = sum(nodeCount *
  (buildWarmupCount + buildReportedRepetitionCount))` over that source's
  linear children, and `shellNodeDirectJ2EvaluationCount` uses the identical
  formula over that source's shell children. These are the two exact global
  ledger counters; there is no separate `nodeDirectJ2EvaluationCount`.
- In `LSC-S6`, every build repetition is disposed except that the final
  reported repetition remains live long enough for its frozen query
  repetitions, one untimed scoring pass against the already computed shared
  holdout authority, and the resource record; it is then disposed. No extra
  node build is permitted for scoring.
- child build timing includes DirectJ2 node-packet construction,
  mapping/topology/accelerator construction, allocation, and materialization;

## Global Run Lifecycle

Every focused-test, deciding, leaf, and aggregate runner follows this exact
order:

1. Read retained paths and collect raw hashes only.
2. Collect the explicit authority, input, implementation, runner, test,
   package-lock, run-record, predecessor-manifest, and transitive primitive
   source set.
3. Call and await `startRunRecord`.
4. Attach interruption handlers.
5. Only now dynamically import validation, tests, physical evaluators, or
   candidate code.
6. Validate retained hashes against the pre-start hashes.
7. Execute through the phase-aware ledger.
8. Construct every additional artifact value in memory and exclusively write
   any payload whose schema does not depend on the final source-stability
   result.
9. Take the final observed-module snapshot, deregister an import observer when
   applicable, then rehash the complete declared source and dynamic-input set
   and require byte equality.
10. Exclusively write the remaining evidence artifacts whose closed schemas
    contain that final source-stability result. Step 10 may deterministically
    assemble, serialize, and hash those artifacts from already-loaded
    in-memory events, counters, snapshots, hashes, and the step-9 stability
    result. It performs no experiment/physical/candidate calculation, local
    module resolution, or source/dynamic-input/protected-path read or
    mutation. Only already-loaded experiment writers and run-record functions
    may write inside the current run directory.
11. Map a frozen criterion miss to `rejected`, infrastructure/execution or
    provenance failure to `failed`, and signals to `interrupted`.
12. Call `finalizeRunRecord` exactly once.
13. Let the finalizer create `artifact-manifest.json` as the final write.
14. Verify the terminal manifest.
15. Exit zero only for `accepted`; rejected/failed is nonzero, and
    interruption uses the signal-appropriate 130 or 143 exit.
16. Allocate a new run for every correction or retry.

A run cannot place proof of its own terminal-manifest verification inside an
artifact covered by that manifest. Any stage exit-gate clause concerning the
current run's terminal manifest is therefore an external post-finalization
acceptance condition performed at step 14, not a pre-finalization
`tables/gates.json` fact. The run is not reusable and does not unlock the next
stage until that external check passes. Predecessor/focused manifests that
already exist may be verified and cited inside the current run.

the shared read-only run-record modules, and its side-effect-free

### Closeout Conclusions

The plan records exactly one conclusion. A rejected earlier stage records its
own conclusion without running Stage 6. A failed/interrupted attempt remains
retryable and is not a family conclusion unless the applicable terminal
authority closes it. If Stage 6 is reached, an authorized aggregate emits
`characterization-complete`, `evidence-invalid`, or `execution-incomplete`.

| Conclusion | Terminal status | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `characterization-complete` | `accepted` | Every validity gate passed and all frozen levels were reported. This selects no cache. |
| `preflight-invalid` | `failed` | The S0 policy, predecessor, protected-baseline, write-boundary, source-closure, zero-counter, or lifecycle evidence was not valid. It is an attempt-level infrastructure/evidence conclusion and may be retried under unchanged plan semantics. |
| `family-input-rejected` | `rejected` | The completed S1 audit found an invalid approved selection, identity, schema, partition, or family freeze. An unresolved selection that prevents S1 from starting is only blocked, not this conclusion. |
| `physical-authority-rejected` | `rejected` | The completed S2 physical source/domain, geometry, symmetry, packet, or DirectJ2-authority calculation failed a frozen gate. |
| `reference-not-converged` | `rejected` | The completed physical-driver, scalar-audit, or shared-monitor calculation failed its frozen rule. |
| `linear-control-structure-rejected` | `rejected` | The completed S3 coordinate-array, interpolation, lookup, or structural packet calculation failed a frozen validity gate. |
| `linear-control-not-converged` | `rejected` | The completed ladder found no retained dense control under the frozen global rule. |
| `shell-structure-rejected` | `rejected` | The completed mapping/topology/lookup calculation failed a frozen structural rule. |
| `realized-family-seal-rejected` | `rejected` | The completed S5 deterministic rebuild, regression-error completeness, lifecycle, source-closure, seal, or campaign calculation failed a frozen gate. |
| `evidence-invalid` | `rejected` | A completed calculation deterministically violates a frozen attribution, campaign-order/non-overlap, completeness, independence, metric, resource-accounting, or timing-work-boundary rule. |
| `execution-incomplete` | `failed` | An explicitly authorized terminal close records that source drift, a missing/corrupt artifact, invalid manifest, infrastructure failure, or interruption prevented valid complete evidence. |

Only `characterization-complete` can accompany an accepted aggregate run. It
still selects no cache size or production candidate.

## Failure And Recovery Rules

| Event | Required disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Focused test or implementation defect before holdout and before the Stage 6 numerical anchor exists; frozen numerical semantics unchanged | Finalize `failed`, fix editable source, rerun focused in a new run, create a new decision binding, and continue. Never edit an old run. |
| Included numerical-source edit after the Stage 6 numerical anchor exists, even before the first leaf or holdout evaluation | Retire the family and create a new family with a fresh holdout. Only the three exact numerical-closure-excluded Stage 6 orchestration paths may retain the family through the strict nonnumerical-repair record. |
| Stage 6 numerical anchor needs any correction | Retire the family and create a new family/holdout. One family has exactly one anchor; do not supersede it. |
| `EPERM`, source drift, or manifest failure | Preserve/finalize the attempt as `failed` when possible; never repair, rename, or reuse its run directory. Before Stage 6, correct under the applicable stage rule and allocate a new run. In Stage 6, follow the separate leaf or aggregate recovery row below according to the failed command. |
| Non-user signal interruption | Preserve/finalize `interrupted`. In Stage 6, follow the separate leaf or aggregate recovery row below. |
| User-requested interruption | Preserve/finalize `interrupted` and wait for an explicit resume; do not retry automatically. On resume, follow the separate leaf or aggregate recovery row below. |
| Completed S0 preflight check miss | Finalize `failed` with `preflight-invalid`, retain every check row, correct only authorized Stage 0 infrastructure/source, and retry in a new run. |
| Completed S1-S5 frozen validity/convergence gate miss | Finalize `rejected`, record the exact stage-specific closeout conclusion, and stop downstream work. Redesign under a new family when the method/input must change. |
| Any family input, method, metric, ladder, packet, topology, or numerical representation changes | Create a new `familyId` and fresh holdout. |
| A level was not frozen in `LSC-S1` | It cannot be appended. Create a new family. |
| Any holdout value has been viewed and numerical source changes, even if called a bug fix | Treat the old holdout as regression; create a new family and fresh holdout. |
| Failed/interrupted leaf, all source bytes unchanged | If terminal-valid, retry that complete source row in a new run under the current anchor-bound decision and supply/omit approval exactly as that decision requires. If allocated-only, initialized-invalid, initialized-unfinalized, or finalized-invalid, obtain the unchanged-retry release, accepted focused run, a new decision bound to the same anchor, and exact approval CLI path first. Reuse no partial authority, child, resource, or timing result. |
| Failed/interrupted aggregate, all leaf/numerical/result bytes unchanged | If terminal-valid with an attempt-level conclusion, prepare the next chained aggregation input and run it once. If allocated-only, initialized-invalid, initialized-unfinalized, or finalized-invalid, first obtain the exact quiescence-only record, then prepare the next chained input. If either exact same-anchor reporting path changes, rerun Stage 6 focused tests and bind a new decision to the same numerical anchor. Any scanner, shared-validator, leaf, numerical, or other runtime-source change requires a new family unless its separately defined leaf-repair rule applies. |
| Out-of-order or overlapping Stage 6 leaf allocation | Keep every attempt, make every order-invalid attempt noncanonical, and use the first issue as `deterministic-campaign-order-rejection` authority for closure-only. Do not continue later leaves or reuse contended timing/resource evidence. |
| Duplicate, stale-input, out-of-order, or overlapping aggregate allocation | Keep every aggregate attempt and use the first deterministic aggregate attribution/order issue for closure-only. A conforming guard prevents this state; do not choose one competing closeout by timing. |
| Post-allocation Stage 6 excluded-orchestration source correction | Default to a new family. Retain the family only if the strict excluded-orchestration-source approval passes, then rerun Stage 6 focused tests and create a new Stage 6 decision before the complete leaf retry. |
| Terminal-valid rejected Stage 6 leaf | Stop later leaves. Use that manifest-verified result as `terminal-valid-evidence-rejection` authority for a closure-only aggregate; do not retry it as orchestration. |
| Source row already has an accepted leaf | Do not rerun for improved output. The earliest valid accepted attempt remains canonical. |
| Campaign binding needs any correction, before or after leaf allocation | Retire the family and create a new family/holdout. One family has exactly one campaign token; do not hide attempts under a replacement token. |
| Post-holdout reporting-only defect with all numerical/result bytes unchanged and no authoritative terminal family conclusion | Create the next chained aggregation-input version by rescanning all leaf and prior aggregate attempts. A terminal-valid failed/interrupted aggregate with an attempt-level conclusion is already quiescent; an invalid/unfinalized aggregate requires the exact quiescence-only record. If either exact same-anchor reporting path changes, rerun Stage 6 focused tests and create a new decision bound to the same numerical anchor. Any scanner, shared-validator, leaf, numerical, or other runtime-source change requires a new family unless its separately defined leaf-repair rule applies. A new aggregate run may reuse verified canonical leaf results but must change no numerical claim. Once any authoritative terminal family conclusion exists, this plan permits no same-family correction or new aggregate input; preserve the terminal evidence and require a separately authorized follow-up. |
| Aggregation preparation repeatedly fails before any aggregation input or aggregate run exists | Preparation itself is not a terminal-close target. Unless an independently qualifying leaf/aggregate attempt already exists, remain nonterminal as `execution-infrastructure-blocked`; correct and retry preparation. |
| Allocation or timing execution failure | Mark `failed`, not “cache too large.” The initial study has no size ceiling. |
| High error, nonmonotone error, large bytes, or slow timing | Report it and continue the frozen campaign. |

Every earlier run remains immutable evidence.
